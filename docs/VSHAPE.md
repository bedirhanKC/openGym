# V-shape Home

A three-day starter programme for two dumbbells, an EZ bar and a stable flat bench or support. No incline bench, pull-up bar or band is required. Load it through Plan → Load starter plan → V-shape Home. It adds ordinary editable routines and uses the existing progression engine.

Monday A, Wednesday B and Friday C are defaults; move them to any three nonconsecutive days. Keep recovery days between sessions. Each session includes lower-body work, with extra rows and lateral raises for the back and shoulders. Existing exercise animations and instructions are used.

## Starting and progressing

- In the first two weeks, reduce exercises prescribed for three sets to two sets in the routine editor. Build up to the listed volume when technique and recovery are comfortable.
- Choose loads that leave approximately 2–3 good repetitions in reserve. No fixed kilogram target is assumed: the available dumbbell and plate weights were not specified.
- The programme uses double progression: increase repetitions within the range, then add the smallest available load after reaching the top on every work set. Configure your actual dumbbell inventory in Settings so suggestions match what you own.
- Compounds rest for two minutes; smaller shoulder and arm movements rest for 75 seconds. Rest longer if needed.
- Split-squat repetitions are per leg. Use no external load initially if needed.
- Bench press needs a stable flat support suitable for lifting. Where unavailable, swap it for the catalogue's dumbbell lying-on-floor chest press. Never use an incline exercise as its replacement.
- Before working sets, take a short general warm-up and perform light practice sets for the first loaded movements. Warm-up sets are separate from the listed work sets.

## Why this programme

The ACSM's 2026 position stand supports regular, high-effort resistance training involving all major muscle groups, and notes that home training can be effective. Its practical hypertrophy guidance places emphasis on sufficient weekly volume; approximately ten sets per muscle group is a useful eventual reference, not a compulsory starting dose. Here rows and lateral raises each provide nine direct weekly sets at the full prescription, with additional assistance from other movements. The precise split is our practical application of this evidence, not a tested or endorsed named ACSM programme.

Rows cover back work within the available equipment. Lateral raises emphasize the side deltoids, while rear-delt fly and overhead press avoid treating the shoulders as a single movement. Resistance-band pulldowns could be an optional future addition after obtaining a band and a suitable anchor, but this starter does not require purchasing one.
